# Data products need a little more honesty

Data products are persuasive by default. A number in a large typeface can feel more certain than the evidence behind it. Good design has to close that gap.

## Context is part of the number

A metric without a time range, comparison, or sample size is only half a statement. The missing half is usually where the decision lives.

Showing context does not make a dashboard less confident. It makes the confidence legible.

## Uncertainty should have a shape

Not every unknown needs a warning label. Sometimes a range, a last-updated timestamp, or a short note about coverage is enough. The goal is not to make people distrust the data; it is to help them use it at the right level of precision.

## Make the next question obvious

The most useful view is rarely the one with the most charts. It is the one that makes the next investigation easy. A good data product leaves a trail from signal to source, from source to assumption, and from assumption to action.

Honesty is not decoration around the analysis. It is part of the interface.
