This is a retrospective written in September 2026 about work and learning across 2025. The label covers January through June; the portfolio records the clearest dated events in April and June rather than a day-by-day diary.

In April, I spent three weeks as a data analytics intern at Infinitea, analysing customer-ordering data and weekly sales reports. The task connected peak hours and best-selling items with inventory and stock planning. In June, I co-founded a mathematics club at school and began building a recurring space for number theory, complex vectors, combinatorics, and IOQM or AMC preparation.

## A pattern needs a next action

The internship work was not complete when a trend appeared in a report. The useful question was what that pattern could mean for stock planning. A peak hour or popular item becomes relevant when someone can connect it to a decision they need to make.

That is a small but durable idea for data products: show enough context to make the next question easier. A report should help someone investigate or act, not just admire a chart.

## Learning grows when it is shared

The mathematics club asks a different version of the same question. A topic becomes more useful when another person can pick it up, test an idea, and ask the next question. The club grew from three to more than twenty active members, with sessions and preparation around the topics listed in my [achievements](/achievements).

Looking back, these were very different settings: one tied observations to operations, the other made mathematical problem-solving communal. Both rewarded clarity about who needs the information and what they can do next.
